# Preference Improvement Lab: results, 9 November 2026

## Decision

Explicit activity clarification improves the first recommendation on these controlled probes. An LLM choosing the question did not improve aggregate accuracy over deterministic clarification with E5. Use local clarification by default, allow a second optional clarification when two independent ambiguities remain, and retain LLM selection as an experiment.

This is evidence about a bounded, authored preference mechanism. It is not a real-user satisfaction percentage, a real-venue accuracy estimate, or a measurement of route/order computation.

## Implementation and actual execution

- Separate `C67PreferenceLab` SwiftUI application, bundle `giovanchristoffel.c67-preference-lab`, deployed to the booted iPhone 18 Pro Max Simulator, iOS 27.2, Xcode 27.2. Deployment target iOS 26.
- Independent local Mac service on loopback port 8780. Existing Context Lab app, source and port 8778 are not replaced.
- Explicit activity/relationship preferences, required versus preferred strength, temporary versus remembered scope, saved choices, local persistence and undo. Opening a card does not infer a fine-grained preference. Further examples and user-initiated clarification remain available.
- Real local E5 through the existing pinned ONNX runtime package, with English authored input. Optional real OpenRouter question selection, minimized input, a disclosure, validation and local fallback.
- Actual Simulator HTTP path to the new service: an AI clarification and live Google Places search were exercised through UI tests. Place results are attributed, not reranked or fed to the LLM/E5. Search results do not verify all activity requirements.

## Experimental design and fairness boundary

There are 18 distinct cases, not 114 independent users. Twelve are six opposite-intent pairs: reading on-site/buying books; coffee together/separate; exhibits/architecture; sunset/daylight; lower/flexible spending; low/more walking. Within each pair, broad interests and candidate order are identical; only the evaluator's intended activity differs.

Six further cases combine two ambiguities. They deliberately test the failure of a one-question policy. All candidates are owned synthetic experience descriptions with neutral IDs and a deterministic shuffled order. They are not facts copied from Google, a venue catalogue, a participant sample or a genuine unseen real-world holdout. The implemented vocabulary and authored probes are related, so perfect performance here must not be generalized.

The candidate matcher, facts and renderer are shared across arms. A receives broad interests; B adds a rule-selected question; C adds a validated LLM-selected question. Known-button answers are supplied by the evaluator's frozen brief, not invented by an LLM. The private brief, expected IDs and answer key are not present in advisor requests. Preference matching and LLM question selection are separate from venue discovery and route optimization.

The E5 comparison uses the same actual Simulator score audit. One-question variants have the same answer budget. The two-round arm is a separate ablation with more interaction, not an equal-effort win. It was measured without E5. Source/fixture hashes, raw observations and the offline replay remain available.

## Main result

“First result correct” means the first ID is in the case's independent expected-ID list. “Complete intent” means all case-specific activity dimensions were explicitly captured. A broad-interest state necessarily cannot contain those finer answers. P@3 counts matching results divided by three, retaining failed/empty outputs; the mixed cases have only two fully relevant candidates, so their maximum P@3 is 2/3.

| Method | Cases | First result correct | Complete intent | P@3 | Additional answers |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Broad interests, no E5 | 18 | 6/18 (33.3%) | 0/18 | 40.7% | 0 |
| Broad interests + E5 | 18 | 8/18 (44.4%) | 0/18 | 42.6% | 0 |
| One local clarification, no E5 | 18 | 14/18 (77.8%) | 12/18 | 81.5% | 1 |
| One local clarification + E5 | 18 | 15/18 (83.3%) | 12/18 | 83.3% | 1 |
| One LLM-selected clarification + E5, with explicit fallback | 18 | 15/18 (83.3%) | 12/18 | 83.3% | 1 |
| Up to two local clarifications, no E5 | 18 | 18/18 (100%) | 18/18 | 88.9% | 1–2; mean 1.33 |

Broad interests + E5 versus one local clarification + E5 improves first-result matching by seven of 18 cases, or 38.9 percentage points in this dataset. The E5 ablation adds one correct first result to the one-question local method here; that small count is not proof of broad E5 superiority.

| Case group | Broad + E5 | One local + E5 | One advisor + E5 | Up to two local |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 12 single-ambiguity cases | 6/12 | 12/12 | 12/12 | 12/12 |
| 6 double-ambiguity cases | 2/6 | 3/6 | 3/6 | 6/6 |

This result supports capturing an activity relationship explicitly. It does not demonstrate that freely generating more questions is beneficial. An answer to one dimension leaves a second independent dimension unknown.

## LLM execution, cost boundary and latency

C produced 16 actual validated Crouton Alpha decisions. Two final cases used local fallback after the campaign limit was reached; they are included in the 18-case result, not described as LLM successes. On the 16 actual LLM cases, first-result matching was 14/16; that subset is not directly comparable with all 18 B cases.

The median advisor processing duration over the 16 actual LLM responses was **1.559 seconds**, maximum **3.287 seconds**. The median including the two immediate budget fallbacks was 1.475 seconds. The former is the appropriate number when explaining the added AI wait. This is service processing, not human answer time or full mobile-task latency. The first local examples do not wait for the advisor.

Local in-process first-result matching used a tiny eight-candidate pool per case and precomputed embeddings. Median broad+E5 matching was 0.055 ms, one local clarification+E5 was 0.245 ms, and up-to-two local clarification was 0.404 ms in the campaign. These figures exclude E5 inference, UI rendering, persistence, provider discovery and human thought; do not compare them with historical multi-stop route benchmarks or treat the ratios as product speedups.

The Simulator E5 audit loaded the actual pinned model with 384 dimensions, ONNX Runtime 1.24.2 / CPU / int8. It measured 0.708 s for initialization/integrity checks and 0.201 s for the audit encoding phase covering 72 authored passages and 30 query variants. A cached pass over the queries took 0.000233 s. This is one Simulator audit, not physical-iPhone latency.

All provider requests enforced zero maximum prompt/completion/request prices. Successful reported token costs were zero; missing cost fields and failed attempts are not fabricated cost receipts. The counter ended at **39 attempted model requests** including development UI diagnosis, the main comparison and the corrected legacy control. Initial cap 30 was reached; it was deliberately extended to 42 solely to correct the comparison, retaining the first run.

## Current-code contract diagnostic and correction

The supplementary control reads the current app's system prompt from `OpenRouterClient.swift` and mirrors `CandidatePolicy`'s category-diversity selection. It runs on the first six predetermined owned probe cases. It does not reproduce the old app's Google discovery, original venue descriptions, UI, full model-state history or route planner. Therefore it must not be labeled the old app's overall accuracy.

The first control incorrectly shared the new advisor's eight-second budget. Only 1/6 calls produced a valid ranking; those observations remain in `live.json`. They are unsuitable for a failure-rate claim about the old implementation.

The corrected control restores the old 17-second combined deadline, reserving up to four seconds for fallback, and its original provider parameter shape. It produced 3/6 valid rankings. The other three returned JSON-decode failure and then transport timeout on fallback. Overall median client duration was 11.482 s. The three available first results did not match the evaluator's hidden brief in this small run. Do not turn these missing/limited observations into a general “0% accuracy” claim.

The corrected evidence still shows that scoring candidates against broad interests does not acquire the missing activity intention. The controlled A/B comparison is the evidence for the preference mechanism; this six-case diagnostic is supporting technical evidence only.

## Verification, failures and repairs

- **10 Swift core tests passed:** paired-input consistency, private-answer exclusion, clarification, two ambiguities, unknown versus contradiction, required-strength preservation during correction, scope, serialization, skip/cap, unsupported answer and stale-result rejection.
- **9 Python service tests passed:** minimal payload, hidden-field rejection, modified-question injection rejection, no unnecessary model request, invalid-model-output fallback, provider failure, empty content, budget enforcement and preservation of zero-price/data policy across fallback.
- **Five UI scenarios have successful execution evidence:** choose/save/restore; E5 audit; actual advisor plus live Places; new-trip scope; skip/local use. An additional targeted check verifies undo after relaunch.
- The first UI suite had a failed live scenario. Provider empty-content handling was expanded so it cannot be misclassified as bad user input; the short advisor response budget was increased from 450 to 1,200 tokens with low reasoning effort. The live test also waits/scrolls for lazy List rows before counting returned place names. The targeted live retest passed.
- After session/history and correction changes, the three affected state UI scenarios were rerun and passed. Required activity strength is preserved when changing its chosen value.
- Every main-case result was reproduced offline from recorded advisor decisions and the real E5 audit after the core fix. The independent report script rechecks fixture hashes, result membership, uniqueness, recomputed metrics and replay agreement.

The original failed debug result bundles remain private and ignored. There is no participant or physical-device validation in these results. The initial provider diagnosis was not a controlled experiment and is excluded from accuracy denominators.

## Recommendation and next evidence

Ship the local clarification mechanism as this PoC's default: broad interests, one relevant activity question, and a second optional question when needed. Keep the editable understanding summary, explicit correction, saved selections and per-trip scope. The LLM has not earned mandatory placement in this bounded flow.

The next substantive uncertainty is human usefulness on fresh briefs and whether permitted real-venue evidence supports the requested activity. Test those before expanding the vocabulary, adding model calls or claiming travel satisfaction. A fluent question cannot supply missing venue facts.
